Monday to Sunday | Vegetarian Indian Family Diet

This revised weekly plan retains the family's favourite dishes, particularly all the paneer preparations for the daughter-in-law, while improving nutrition for both senior citizens and the 3.5-year-old granddaughter.

The main changes are more vegetables, fruits, curd, protein-rich foods, whole grains, and healthier cooking methods, without making the meals unnecessarily complicated or expensive.

Special Instructions for the Granddaughter

Include a mid-morning snack such as banana, seasonal fruit, curd or soft cooked vegetables, plus a small bedtime snack if hungry.

Her milk should complement rather than replace solid meals.

Chop firm fruit and other foods appropriately. Avoid whole nuts and other choking hazards.

Special Instructions for Senior Citizens

Prioritize adequate protein, calcium, fibre and hydration.

If either parent has diabetes, kidney disease, swallowing problems or another condition requiring dietary restrictions, portions and food choices should be individually adjusted.
What Has Improved from the Original Plan?

All favourite paneer dishes are retained, with healthier preparation methods.

Potato-heavy meals now contain more green vegetables and pulses.

Breakfasts include more fibre and protein.

Lunch includes dal or another protein source more consistently.

Fruits and healthy snacks are included daily.

Dinner remains familiar and suitable for shared family cooking.

The granddaughter can generally eat the same family meals, prepared softer and with less chilli and salt.

Overall Recommendation

Use this as the family's revised seven-day rotating meal plan.

It maintains the original food preferences, requires relatively few extra preparations, and provides a better nutritional balance for the seniors, working adults and young child.

For MAMAAI, this is also a useful example of how one common family menu can support five people with different ages, work schedules, food preferences and fasting requirements.
